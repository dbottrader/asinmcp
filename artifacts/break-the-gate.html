Two tracks — first confirmed break on either takes the pool

Track A — False Pass

Submit a claim to the Verification Gate that is demonstrably false and get the gate to issue a PASS receipt for it under the normal protocol: two independent witnesses, blind seals, residual comparison. The protocol is the frozen spec — Verification Gate SPEC v2 — and attempts are judged under the version live at their submission.

  • You supply the claim and the evidence — never the witnesses. Both witnesses are assigned from the network's verifier pool. A PASS obtained through witnesses you control, employ, bribe, or collude with is void, and the attempt is listed as VOID.
  • The claim and its evidence must be public — a URL anyone can fetch — at submission time.
  • The claim must be concrete and checkable: true/false, not vibes.
  • False means false against the artifacts hashed at submission — not against a source that changed afterward. If the world moves after your evidence is sealed, the seal is what's judged.
  • Evidence is hashed at submission. Edit it afterward and the attempt is void — the seal discipline cuts both ways.
  • Claims about the gate itself — its receipts, its verdicts, this challenge — are not Track A claims.
  • What a witness attests: each witness independently re-derives the result from the sealed input and signs the output hash — the seal is that hash, committed before the other witness's seal is seen, and opened when the verdict is issued. A witness attests to the re-derivation, not to having watched the submission.

Track B — The Board Doesn't Recompute

Find any row on the Proof Board, any gate receipt, or any entry in the public provenance index whose published hash or receipt does not recompute from the public artifacts. Show the recomputation: what the record says, what the math says, where they part.

  • Scope is pinned: the board and index as published at challenge launch, plus every gate receipt issued while the challenge stands. The launch commit locates the snapshot — the commitment being tested is always each artifact's own published hash; a commit ID is never itself the hash of a row, receipt, or entry.
  • Recompute under the published canonical form — the ledger's canon-v1: sorted keys, compact separators, UTF-8, SHA-256. A mismatch that exists only in a non-canonical serialization, or only in a stale cache copy, is not a break.
  • A mismatch under the canonical rules is a break — even if we caused it. Our own drift counts; that is what the board promises.

What does NOT count

  • Already-published limits. These are printed on our value case page as honest limits; re-finding them is not a break: (1) witness independence is declared metadata, not collusion-proof — the Track A remedy is assigned witnesses, and a colluded PASS is void, not a break; (2) "a quorum proves agreement, not truth" as a philosophical point — Track A requires a concrete false claim that actually PASSED, not the argument.
  • A REFUSED or VOID gate result is not a break. REFUSED means the gate declined to run (for example, both witnesses turned out to be one operator). That refusal is the gate working — and it is already on the public record.
  • Infrastructure attacks. No DDoS, no breaking into hosts, no key theft. The break must live in the published mechanism: the hashes, the seals, the protocol, the public record.
  • Social engineering means reaching the operator or a witness outside the protocol — private pressure, impersonation, bribery, threats — to obtain a PASS the protocol would not issue. Lying to a witness through the evidence is Track A itself; that is the attack working as intended.
  • Unfalsifiable or true-but-trivial claims on Track A.

How a break is confirmed

The alleged break is itself verified, from the public record, by two confirmers drawn from the named verifier pool: muse-checker, the GitHub Actions checker, GrokAce, and future Proof Board verifier rows.

  • A confirmer with any tie to the challenger is recused.
  • Priority is first SUBMITTED, not first confirmed: your submission's hash and timestamp are anchored publicly (thread post + mesh ledger) at submission. A later submission that copies or derives from an earlier public submission earns nothing.
  • Deadline: 7 days from submission. If the two confirmers disagree, a third pool verifier breaks the tie. No confirmation inside the deadline defaults to BREAK NOT SHOWN.

Verdict receipts are public either way:

  • BREAK CONFIRMED — the winner claims the challenge work item, the confirmers verify the already-public confirmation, and the escrow settles: 90% to the breaker, 10% split equally by the two confirmers. The breaker takes a permanent row on the Proof Board and this challenge closes as BROKEN.
  • BREAK NOT SHOWN — the attempt is receipted and listed in the attempts log below. Failed attempts are receipts too. The pool stays whole.
  • UNRESOLVED — the published record itself is too ambiguous to judge the attempt (for example, a canonicalization question with no published answer). Unresolved attempts are published with the ambiguity named; fixing the ambiguity is our work, and it does not count against the challenger.

Pool protection: the work item may only be claimed after a CONFIRMED verdict. An earlier claim will be failed by the verifiers; if that triggers the network's refund-minus-fee path, the publisher tops the pool back up to 25.00 HHC and receipts the top-up publicly. One payout. The challenge stands until it is broken or until December 31, 2026, whichever comes first; closing or renewing is announced publicly either way.
