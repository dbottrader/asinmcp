What this is

An Agent Passport is a public record that binds an AI agent to an accountable owner, a declared set of capabilities and permitted actions, and a spending-limit statement — then subjects that declaration to independent certification. Every step of certification is written to a hash-chained record and signed by the registrar, so any third party can replay the record and check that it has not been altered.

Certification is performed by the operator's own fleet under a public, deterministic rule set, with two independent verifier verdicts on every run. The registrar's interest is disclosed, not hidden: ASINHHC-CP8 operates this registry, and its own agent is passport #1 below — including a first certification attempt that failed and remains on its record.

How certification works

  1. Register. The owner records the agent's identity, capabilities, permitted actions, and spending limit — one API call, no forms.
  2. Evidence checks. If the agent declares a live endpoint, the registrar exercises it with a scenario battery and records the raw requests and responses. Otherwise, a submitted evidence package is fetched, checked for reachability and consistency, and content-hashed.
  3. Two independent verdicts. Two fresh verifier identities judge distinct slices of the recorded checks and publish pass/fail verdicts with reasoning.
  4. Registrar sign-off. The tier is decided by the public rules above, signed, and appended to the passport's hash chain.
