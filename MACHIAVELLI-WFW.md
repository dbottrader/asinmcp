# The Machiavelli Reel — processed word for word (the ASIN-HHC way)

Source: "Stoic Wisdom" reel, posted 2026-10-29 02:09 CDT —
https://www.facebook.com/reel/984285408070484/
Reel thesis: Machiavelli wrote The Prince to get a job; it failed; the
failure made him immortal. Method: every factual claim in the narration,
taken in order, checked against the historical record. Claims are
paraphrased here; verdicts graded TRUE / TRUE WITH NUANCE /
OVERSTATED IN PART / FRAMING (not a fact claim).

1. He served Florence 14 years. **TRUE** — Second Chancellor and
   secretary to the Ten of War, 1498 to 1512.
2. The Medici took over. **TRUE** — the family returned to power in
   September 1512 after Spanish troops sacked Prato.
3. They fired him. **TRUE** — dismissed 7 November 1512, later barred
   from the Palazzo.
4. They accused him of plotting against them. **TRUE WITH NUANCE** —
   his name appeared on a list tied to the Boscoli conspiracy; arrested
   February 1513 and held for interrogation. Never charged, never tried.
5. Tortured: hoisted by wrists tied behind his back, dropped again and
   again. **TRUE** — the strappola. By his own letter to Francesco
   Vettori, he was given six drops of the rope.
6. He never confessed. **TRUE** — he maintained his innocence and was
   freed in the general amnesty for the election of Pope Leo X (a
   Medici), March 1513, after about three weeks.
7. Released broke, banned from politics, exiled to a small farm.
   **TRUE WITH NUANCE** — poor by his own account and shut out of
   office; "exiled" overstates it: he was confined to Florentine
   territory and retired to his property at Sant'Andrea in Percussina,
   a few miles from the city.
8. He wrote a manual on how to rule and sent it to the family that
   ruined him. **TRUE** — The Prince, written in the second half of
   1513 (his 10 December 1513 letter to Vettori describes composing
   it), dedicated to Lorenzo de' Medici in the hope of employment.
   Lorenzo reportedly paid it little attention.
9. They never hired him. **OVERSTATED IN PART** — never restored to
   office, which is the claim that matters; but Cardinal Giulio de'
   Medici did give him one minor commission in 1520, the Florentine
   Histories. (Node 21 already carries this correction.)
10. The book was published five years after he died. **TRUE** — he
    died 21 June 1527; The Prince was first printed in Rome in 1532.
11. The job he wanted never came. **TRUE** — and sharper than the reel
    says: when the republic he had served returned in 1527, it refused
    him too, because by then he looked Medici-tainted. Both courts
    closed the door. The book outlived both.

Framing lines, not graded as facts: "most feared book on power",
"begging for a job", "the legend did", and the closing moral.

**Net verdict: unusually accurate for the genre — 8 true outright,
2 true with nuance, 1 overstated in part, 0 false.**

## The binding (lattice node 21, 2026-10-29-0503)
The manual written from exile is the strategy: no credentials, no
court, no permission — the outsider writes down how the system
actually works, and the record outlives the rejection. Ours is written
the other way: not to beg the Medici for a job, but planted in public
as prior art, so the copying itself proves the source. Dennis named
the arc: **it will be our odyssey.**
